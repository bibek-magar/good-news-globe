🐬 6 baby Irrawaddy dolphins were born on Cambodia's Mekong River, lifting the population to 118 + 3 more wins you missed 👇

1. 🐬 6 baby dolphins were born on Cambodia's Mekong River
This calving season brought 6 Irrawaddy dolphin calves, taking the group on a 75-mile stretch of the Mekong to 118. It is the largest population of the species still living in a river.
🔗 Good News Network: https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/numbers-of-endangered-and-sacred-river-dolphin-grow-by-6-after-successful-breeding-season/

2. 🦎 The 1st amphibian known to live fully at sea was found
No living amphibian survives in the ocean. But 2 near-complete skeletons from China show Gerrothorax maritimus, an armoured, 3-foot bottom-dweller, lived its whole life at sea.
🔗 Phys.org: https://phys.org/news/2026-10-armored-amphibian-sea-million-years.html

3. 🧹 Told to 'go clean it yourself', a New Yorker did
Joshua Service filmed a dumping site under a rail bridge. Commenters dared him to fix it, so he came back with gloves and a rake and filled 10 trash bags in about 2 hours.
🔗 Good News Network: https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/man-named-service-cleans-up-nyc-dumping-site-on-his-own-initiative/

4. ⛪ Islanders crowdfunded a dig and found a lost monastery
Many of Lismore's 170 residents paid for a dig at their church. It revealed a 6th-century monastery with burials, carved crosses and crucibles likely used for gold and silver.
🔗 Good News Network: https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/tiny-scottish-islands-little-historical-society-makes-massive-discovery/

✨ Fun fact: Irrawaddy dolphins have no beak: their round, blunt heads make them look more like small belugas.
