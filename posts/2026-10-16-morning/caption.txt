🟢 Astronomers found the 1st green star ever: a dead star tinted green by sodium from old planets + 3 more wins you missed 👇

1. 🟢 Astronomers found the 1st green star ever seen
White dwarf WD J1330+6435 swallowed rocky debris from its old planets. Sodium in its dense atmosphere absorbs orange light, so to human eyes it would glow a ghostly green.
🔗 Space.com: https://www.space.com/astronomy/stars/astronomers-discover-the-1st-ever-green-star-a-cosmic-zombie-just-in-time-for-halloween

2. 🐙 A deep-sea canyon off Argentina held 57 new species
Taxonomists found 57 new species and 5 new genera, including a horned sea cucumber nicknamed Loki, 2,577 m down. Of the copepods checked, every single one was new to science.
🔗 Mongabay (@mongabay): https://news.mongabay.com/2026/10/patrick-sea-star-went-viral-but-argentine-deep-sea-canyon-held-57-new-species/

3. 👁️ A gene therapy helped blind patients find objects again
In a trial for advanced retinitis pigmentosa, one injection made surviving eye cells sense light. With special goggles, 7 of 10 patients saw more light, and some found doors and objects.
🔗 AllSci: https://allsci.com/news/clinical-trials/optogenetic-therapy-for-advanced-retinitis/

4. 🐢 A 194-year-old tortoise has gene switches that look young
Scientists read the DNA of Jonathan, the oldest known land animal. Gene switches for energy and DNA repair looked like a 5-year-old tortoise's, and he carries 287 unusual gene variants.
🔗 Popular Mechanics: https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/a74103821/jonathan-the-tortoise/

✨ Fun fact: Jonathan arrived on St Helena in 1882, already fully grown.
