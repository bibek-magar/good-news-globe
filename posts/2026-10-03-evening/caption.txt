🐘 Wild elephants rumble in chorus, and AI shows their calls sync up as they plan their next move + 3 more wins 👇

1. 🐘 Elephants 'sing' in chorus to agree on what to do next
Using machine learning on 902 recordings of 84 wild elephants, scientists found rumbles in group choruses grow more alike as elephants greet each other or plan their next move.
🔗 Phys.org: https://phys.org/news/2026-10-elephant-choruses-rumbles-groups.html

2. 🧲 Indian physicists find a superconductor that breaks the rules
A team at IISER Bhopal grew crystals of YbSb₂ and probed them with muons. It's the first 'type I' superconductor seen making its own tiny magnetic fields as it starts to superconduct.
🔗 Phys.org: https://phys.org/news/2026-10-scientists-superconductor-reversal-symmetry.html

3. 🪙 20 English coins reveal medieval Rome's 'Little England'
Digs beside the Vatican found 19 silver pennies and a halfpenny from 10th-century England, plus craft workshops: the first physical trace of an English quarter that may have housed hundreds.
🔗 Arkeonews (@arkeo_news): https://arkeonews.net/anglo-saxon-coins-found-near-the-vatican-reveal-romes-lost-little-england/

4. 🦗 A hand-sized insect bounced back from just 1 island
By the 1990s wētāpunga, NZ's heaviest insect, survived only on Little Barrier Island. Zoos then bred tens of thousands, and last year 11,000 went to predator-free islands and sanctuaries.
🔗 RNZ: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/regions_auckland/1693089/big-boom-for-wetapunga-population-celebrated

✨ Fun fact: About 60% of elephant rumbles happen in choruses, and parts dip below what human ears can hear.
