🐢 Sea turtles just nested in California for the first time on record, and 204 eggs were rushed to safety + 3 more wins you missed 👇

1. Sea turtles nest on California beaches for the first time
Two olive ridley turtles laid eggs at Seal Beach and Huntington Beach, a first for the US West Coast. Before storms hit, wildlife teams moved 204 eggs to incubators at the Aquarium of the Pacific.
Source: Good News Network (https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/first-ever-recorded-sea-turtle-nests-on-americas-west-coast/)

2. A 'Cretaceous rabbit' fossil solves a 40-year mystery
Tamirkhan, a 6–7 inch mammal that lived alongside dinosaurs, is the most complete zhelestid ever found. Described in Nature, it shows this group wasn't related to today's placental mammals after all.
Source: Good News Network (https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/new-gobi-desert-fossil-rewrites-a-chapter-of-mammal-evolution/)

3. A snake missing since 1872 turns up on a road in India
A forest patrol near Bhuj photographed an Afro-Asian sand snake, the first confirmed record in Kutch in over 150 years. The last specimens were collected by naturalist Ferdinand Stoliczka in 1871–72.
Source: India Today (https://www.indiatoday.in/science/story/kutch-rare-snake-sighting-afro-asian-sand-wildlife-habitat-3006242-2026-09-30)

4. Two Brazilian birds scream louder than 120 decibels
Biologists found that the bare-throated bellbird and the red-legged seriema both break 120 decibels, joining the white bellbird, clocked at 125 dB in 2019. That's louder than a jackhammer.
Source: WBUR (https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/29/umass-amherst-worlds-loudest-birds-bellbird-seriema)

✨ Fun fact: Red-legged seriemas sing in duets, pairs trading calls back and forth like a squabbling couple.
