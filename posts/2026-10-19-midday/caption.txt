🏃‍♀️ At 93, sprinter Emma Mazzenga has muscle fibres as big as a woman in her 20s, scientists found + 3 more wins 👇

1. 🏃‍♀️ At 93, a sprinter's muscle fibres match a woman in her 20s
Emma Mazzenga holds the over-90 world record for 200 m. Scientists found her fitness level matches someone in their 50s, and her fatigue-resistant muscle fibres are as big as a 20-something's.
🔗 CBC News (@cbc): https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/emma-mazzenga-italian-sprinter-longevity-9.7383784

2. 🌋 After a 7,300-year-old eruption, some ancient Japanese thrived
The undersea Kikai-Akahoya eruption buried Japan in ash. A new study finds Jomon survivors moved to the northern Kyushu coast, turned to the sea for food, and their population kept growing.
🔗 ScienceDaily: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261017230023.htm

3. 🦍 Bili, a gorilla seized from traffickers, is heading back to the wild
Rescued as a sick baby in a 2023 sting in Nigeria, Bili was flown to a Jane Goodall Institute sanctuary in Congo. She raced into the forest and climbed trees. The plan: a gorilla friend, then the wild.
🔗 CNN (@cnn): https://www.cnn.com/science/bili-gorilla-relocation-nigeria-republic-of-congo-c2e-spc

4. 🪸 Ghost-white corals fully recovered in 7 weeks, a record
Stanford scientists let bleached corals touch healthy clones from the same colony. Their tissues fused, life-giving algae flowed in, and the bleached corals fully recovered. Those not touching died.
🔗 Good News Network (@goodnewsnetwork): https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/coral-bleaching-reversed-at-record-speed-using-new-stanford-method/

✨ Fun fact: The Kikai-Akahoya eruption was the largest volcanic eruption on Earth in the last 10,000 years.
